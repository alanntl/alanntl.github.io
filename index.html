      04 — Research

      Research.

      Water science at Western Sydney University, and reinforcement learning applied to problems where the interesting question is which inputs break the solver.

        Experiment

        One site, two editions.

        Every visit opens as either the night river or the parchment. Which one you get is a draw — and the odds are learned from you, by you, on your own device. This page is the experiment, running.

        Your draw

        Next visit's odds.

        The belief

        What your browser thinks.

        Each curve is a Bayesian belief about your average stay in one edition — wide when evidence is thin, sharpening as visits accumulate. The odds are the probability that one true preference beats the other, kept between 10% and 90% — and deliberately tempered while samples are few: with n visits to the lesser-seen edition the comparison carries only √(n/(n+5)) of its weight, so a lucky first visit cannot run away with the draw.

        The ledger

        Your data.

          All of this lives in your browser's storage. There is no server, no analytics, and nothing is sent anywhere — resetting below restarts your experiment at a coin flip.
